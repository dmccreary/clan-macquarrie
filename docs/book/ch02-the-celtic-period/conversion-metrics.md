# MacQuarrie History - Chronological Timeline
## Extracted from Chapters I & II

---

## Ancient Period (Pre-Christian Era)

**Before 503 AD**
- Celtic peoples migrate to Ireland from continental Europe
- Ireland becomes known as "Scotia"
- Celts develop advanced culture in northeastern Ireland
- Druids practice their religion throughout the region

---

## Early Christian & Dalriadic Period

**390 AD**
- St. Ninian comes to Scotland from Rome
- Preaches to the Picts
- Builds small church (later replaced by Abbey)

**503 AD**
- **Fergus mac Ere** leads Scots from Ireland to Scotland
- Brothers Loarn and Angus accompany him
- Found Kingdom of **Dalriada** in Argyll
- Three kingdoms now in Britain: Northern Picts, Southern Picts, and Dalriada

**521 AD**
- **St. Columba** born in Donegal, Ireland
- Family: Father Phelin mac Fergus, Mother Eithne
- Descended from King Niall of the 9 Hostages
- Grand-nephew of Fergus mac Ere

**563 AD**
- St. Columba arrives in Scotland from Ireland
- Given Isle of **Iona** (then called "I") by Dalriadic king
- Establishes monastery, church, and training school

**573 AD**
- Battle of Ardderyd - Christian victory
- St. Kentigern (Mungo) returns to Glasgow

**584 AD**
- St. Columba preaches near river Tay
- Probable meeting with St. Kentigern

**597 AD**
- **Death of St. Columba** on Iona
- Same year St. Augustine lands in southern England
- Celtic Church continues his work for 600 years

---

## Viking Period

**6th-7th Centuries**
- Irish monasteries become famous for learning throughout Europe
- Celtic missionaries spread across Scotland, England, and western Europe

**795 AD**
- First Viking raid on Iona

**801 AD**
- Second Viking raid on Iona

**802 AD**
- Third Viking raid on Iona

**806 AD**
- Fourth Viking raid on Iona

**8th-10th Centuries**
- Norse Vikings rampage throughout northern waters
- Establish control of Hebrides, Orkney, and Shetland
- Create stronghold in Dublin, Ireland
- Scots sometimes ally with English against Norse
- Scots sometimes ally with Norse against English

---

## Formation of Scotland

**836 AD**
- **Alpin** (son of King Achaius and Princess Ferfkusia) killed
- Son Kenneth inherits Dalriadan crown and claim to Pictish throne

**843 AD**
- **Kenneth mac Alpin** claims throne of the Picts
- Unites Picts and Scots by force
- Capital established first at Dunstaffnage, then moved to Scone

**843-859 AD**
- **Reign of Kenneth mac Alpin**
- First King of united Scotland
- **Stone of Destiny (Lia Fail)** placed at Scone
- Picts gradually lose identity, adopt Gaelic language
- Beginning of **Alpinian line**

**850 AD (approximate)**
- **Guaire**, descendant of Kenneth MacAlpin
- Progenitor of **Clan MacQuarrie**
- Brother **Finguine** (Fingon) - progenitor of Clan MacKinnon

**859 AD**
- Death of Kenneth mac Alpin
- First King of united Scotland buried at Iona
- Over next 300 years, 48 Scottish Kings buried there

**859-863 AD**
- **Donald II** reigns (Kenneth's brother)

**863-881 AD**
- **Constantine** reigns (Kenneth's son)

**870 AD**
- Fifth Viking raid on Iona
- Abbot and 86 monks slain on Martyrs' Bay

**881-882 AD**
- **Heth** reigns (Kenneth's son)

## Later Alpinian Kings

**Following Kings (dim in history)**
- Donald the Ruddy
- Hugh the Fair
- Duff the Brown
- Kenneth the Grim
- At least 12 of 16 kings after Kenneth assassinated or killed in battle

**970-994 AD**
- Kenneth III reigns

**986 AD**
- Sixth (and final recorded) Viking raid on Iona

**994-995 AD**
- Constantine reigns

**995-1003 AD**
- Kenneth IV reigns

**1003-1033 AD**
- Malcolm II reigns

**After 1033**
- Duncan succeeds Malcolm II
- Later killed by MacBeth

## MacQuarrie Clan Development

**Early 900s (if Farquharson tradition correct)**
- Clan MacQuarrie traces origin to Guaire, son of Kenneth MacAlpin

**Late 1200s (if Alan Macquarrie theory correct)**
- **Cormac mac Airbertaich** (descendant of Dalriadic rulers)
- Sons: **Guaire** and **Finguine** (Fingon)
- Guaire founds Clan MacQuarrie
- Finguine founds Clan MacKinnon

**12th or 15th Century (disputed)**
- MacDonald sennachie records banquet
- Chiefs seated by rank and station
- **MacQuarrie seated 2nd**
- Praised as "ancient thane" - "the brave, the fearless, the great feated"

**Centuries before 18th Century**
- MacQuarries firmly established on **Isle of Ulva**
- Also hold Staffa, Little Colonsay, and part of Mull
- Known as rough, turbulent fighters
- Allied first with MacDonalds, later with MacLeans of Duart

---

## Related Clans from Alpine Line

All descended from the Alpinian/Alpine race:
- **Clan MacQuarrie** (from Guaire)
- **Clan MacKinnon** (from Finguine/Fingon)
- Clan MacGregor
- Clan MacNaughton
- Clan MacPhie
- Clan Grant
- Clan Macnab
- Clan MacAulay

---

## Later Medieval Period

**1200 AD**
- Roman Christianity predominates over Celtic tradition
- Celtic organizational structure largely replaced

**1249 AD**
- Reference to **Cormac Mhor** (MacQuarrie)
- Killed in battle against Norse
- Sons Alan and Gregor flee to Ireland

**1263 AD**
- King Alexander III defeats Norse at Largs
- Claims Western Isles for Scotland
- Alan (son of Cormac Mhor) returns from Ireland
- Becomes MacQuarrie chief
- Gregor stays in Ireland

**1285 AD**
- Death of Alexander III
- End of Alpinian line (25 kings total from 834-1285)

**1296 AD**
- King Edward I of England takes **Stone of Destiny**
- Removed from Scone to Westminster Abbey in England

## Clan Symbol & Identity

**Emblem**: Pine tree (variety best able to resist strong gale)

**Crest**: Dagger held aloft, ready to strike

**War Cry** (Gaelic): *An far m breac deag*
- Translation: "Here come the red-spattered tartans"

**Motto** (Latin): *Turris Fortis Mihi Deus*
- Translation: "God is a strong tower to me"

**Name Variations**:
- MacQuarrie (most common)
- MacGuarie
- Macquaire
- MacGuidhre (ancient Gaelic form)

**Reputation**: "The most acquisitive raiders who ever purloined barley" - "bad soldiers, but good plunderers" (meaning unwilling to submit to military discipline, not poor fighters)

## Modern Period Events (Brief)

**1688**
- Fire destroys most MacQuarrie family papers
- Leaves clan annals "more dubious than they might otherwise have been"

**1715 & 1745**
- Jacobite uprisings
- English impose repressive measures on clans
- Highlanders forbidden to carry arms, wear kilts, play bagpipes

**1747**
- Hereditary jurisdictions abolished
- Chiefs lose legal position as magistrates
- Status as heads of clans continues

**1715-1818**
- **Lachlan MacQuarrie** - Last hereditary Chief of Ulva
- Lived to age 103

**1761-1824**
- **Major General Lachlan Macquarie**
- Governor of New South Wales, Australia
- Cousin of Chief Lachlan (not father and son)
- Known as "Father of Australia"

**1773**
- James Boswell and Dr. Samuel Johnson visit Ulva
- Chief Lachlan is their "genial host"
- Described as "intelligent, polite and much a man of the world"

**1777**
- Isle of Ulva sold at public auction
- Pays off long-standing mortgages of Chief Lachlan
- End of MacQuarrie ownership after centuries

**1782**
- Ban on Highland dress lifted (after 36 years)

**Post-1777**
- Many MacQuarries remain on Ulva as tenants
- Others emigrate to Canada, United States
- Later migrations to New South Wales and Far East
- Clan scatters across the globe
